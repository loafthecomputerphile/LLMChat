Mathew had always lived a quiet life on the edge of the old harbor, where gulls stitched the sky with their cries and the scent of salt clung to his clothes. Each morning, he walked the same worn path along the pier, greeting the boats as if they were old friends. He took comfort in that routine, in the rhythm of waves tapping the shore, and in the small wooden workshop he kept tucked behind the lighthouse. There, he carved miniature ships — delicate, intricate things that seemed almost ready to sail away.

One autumn morning, a storm rolled in sooner than the forecasts promised. The sea churned in restless shades of steel, and the wind rattled the workshop door as if urging him outside. When Mathew stepped out, he spotted a bottle tangled in seaweed at the water’s edge, a note sealed inside. As he unfolded the damp paper, he found a message asking for help and signed only with the initials “L.R.” Something about it struck a chord deep within him — a call to leave behind the safety of his routines. For the first time in years, he packed a bag, locked up the workshop, and set off down the coast in search of whoever had reached out across the waves.
